Research

Recent and upcoming work

Specifying Faithfulness: Evaluating Algorithmic Explanations in Context

Grant Shanklin, Jiří Němeček, Asia J. Biega. Under submission at ACM FAccT 2027.

Develops a unified framework for specifying and evaluating the faithfulness of algorithmic explanations, showing through adverse action notices in consumer credit how explanatory, institutional, and normative considerations can guide the choice of context-appropriate evaluation criteria.

How Legal AI Architectures Take Sides on Jurisprudential Debates

Grant Shanklin, Claudio Novelli, Giovanni Sartor, Luciano Floridi. Preprint. Under submission at Artificial Intelligence and Law.

Develops a methodology for identifying how legal AI architectures represent knowledge, organize inference, and constrain inputs and outputs, applying it to four architectural families and drawing on debates in legal theory to articulate their implicit assumptions about legal reasoning.

The Case for Contextual Copyleft: Licensing Open-Source Training Data and Generative AI

Grant Shanklin, Emmie Hine, Claudio Novelli, Tyler Schroder, Luciano Floridi. International Journal of Law and Information Technology, 2026.

Introduces the Contextual Copyleft AI (CCAI) licence, a novel mechanism extending copyleft obligations from open-source training data to the generative models trained on it, enhancing developer control and mitigating open-washing.

Open-Source AI Made in the EU: Why it is a Good Idea

Luciano Floridi, Carlotta Buttaboni, Emmie Hine, Claudio Novelli, Tyler Schroder, Grant Shanklin. Minds and Machines, vol. 35, no. 2, pp. 1–10, 2025.

Argues that the EU’s regulatory framework and ethical commitments uniquely position it to produce trustworthy open-source foundation models, offering a competitive alternative to proprietary and foreign AI systems.
